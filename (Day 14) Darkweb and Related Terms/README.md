
## 🌐 Surface Web
- Also called the **Visible Web** or **Indexed Web**.  
- It’s the part of the internet that search engines like Google, Bing, and Yahoo can index.  
- Examples: YouTube, Facebook, Wikipedia, news websites, blogs.  
- Makes up **~5% of the entire internet**.  

---

## 🌊 Deep Web
- The portion of the internet **not indexed by search engines**.  
- Requires specific credentials or direct links to access.  
- Examples:
  - Online banking portals
  - Subscription services (Netflix, research papers, journals)
  - Private company databases
- Makes up **~90–95% of the internet**.  
- The Deep Web itself is **not illegal** — it simply includes private or restricted content.  

---

## 🌑 Dark Web
- A **small portion of the Deep Web** (around 6%–7%).  
- Accessible only with special tools such as **Tor Browser** or **I2P**.  
- Known for:
  - **Illegal activities** (drugs, weapons, hacking services, stolen data, fake IDs).
  - **Legal use cases** (journalists protecting sources, activists in authoritarian regimes).  

---

## 🕸 Tor Browser
- Stands for **The Onion Router**.  
- Free, open-source browser that allows **anonymous communication** by routing traffic through multiple relays.  
- Features:
  - Hides IP addresses.
  - Accesses **.onion** websites (not available on normal browsers).
- Primary tool to access the **Dark Web**.  

---

## 🛒 Silk Road (Dark Web Market)
- Launched in **2011** by **Ross Ulbricht (a.k.a. Dread Pirate Roberts)**.  
- One of the **first and most famous dark web marketplaces**.  
- Specialized in selling **illegal drugs** and contraband.  
- Shut down by the FBI in **2013**, and Ross Ulbricht received a **life sentence**.  

---

## 🔪 Slayers (Assassination & Life-Ruining Services)
- Claimed dark web “service” offering **hitman-for-hire**, **revenge**, and **life-ruining services**.  
- Most of these sites are **scams** designed to trick people into paying Bitcoin.  
- Law enforcement has **never confirmed** the existence of a real, verified assassination marketplace — usually, they’re fake.  

---

## 🃏 Joker’s Stash
- A **darknet marketplace** specializing in **stolen credit card data (CC dumps, CVVs, fullz)**.  
- Operated from **2014–2021**.  
- Generated millions of dollars in illegal profits.  
- Shut down in **February 2021**, announced by its own operators before authorities seized infrastructure.  

---

## 💰 Dark Web Price Index
- An **annual report** (by security companies like Privacy Affairs, Comparitech, etc.) analyzing the **cost of illegal items** on darknet markets.  
- Examples from past reports:
  - Stolen PayPal account (with balance) → $200–$500  
  - Credit card with full details (fullz) → $20–$100  
  - Fake passports → $1,500–$3,000  
  - Drugs, malware, hacking tools also priced.  
- Prices fluctuate depending on **law enforcement crackdowns** and **supply/demand**.  

---

## 🛒 AlphaBay Market
- Launched in **2014**, quickly became **the largest darknet marketplace** after Silk Road.  
- Sold drugs, malware, counterfeit currency, stolen data, etc.  
- Taken down in **2017** in an international operation.  
- The alleged admin, **Alexandre Cazes**, was arrested in Thailand and later found dead in prison.  
- In **2021**, AlphaBay was relaunched by one of the original staff, known as **DeSnake**.  

---

## 🛒 Hansa Market
- Another major darknet marketplace.  
- Became popular after **AlphaBay’s takedown in 2017**.  
- Unknown to users, Dutch police had already seized Hansa **before AlphaBay was shut down**.  
- This led to one of the **biggest darknet stings**, as law enforcement monitored all transactions before finally taking it offline.
